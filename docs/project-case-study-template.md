# Project case-study template

Copy this outline for one project. Replace the prompts with concrete facts and remove sections that add no value. This is an authoring template; it is not rendered by the website yet.

## At a glance

- Title:
- One-sentence summary: what you built or investigated, for whom, and why.
- Dates and current status:
- My role and collaborators:
- Repository / demo / report: include only available links.
- Main result: state what the evidence supports, including an inconclusive outcome.

## The problem

What question or user need started the project? What would success look like? Explain the practical context before introducing the technology.

## Constraints and ownership

What time, data, technical, or access constraints shaped the work? What did you personally design, implement, test, or analyse? Distinguish your contribution from team or existing work.

## How it works

Trace one concrete input through the system to its output. Include a small diagram or screenshot if it clarifies the flow. Explain why each major component exists rather than listing tools.

## Decisions and experiments

Choose two or three decisions that reveal your judgment. For each, explain:

1. What you observed or needed to decide.
2. The plausible options and their tradeoffs.
3. What you chose and why, given the information available then.
4. What you tried and what happened.
5. What the result changed in your understanding or implementation.

Use evidence where available: a before/after example, an experiment table, a failure case, a design sketch, or a relevant code excerpt. A short account of a consequential failed approach can be more informative than a list of features.

## Results and evidence

What worked, and how did you check it? Give the baseline or comparison, evaluation setup, relevant dates/sample size, and metric definitions for numerical claims. Link supporting artifacts where possible.

For research or backtests, separate observed results from conclusions about future performance. For unfinished work, explain what has actually been demonstrated so far.

## Limitations and lessons

What remains unresolved? Where does the approach fail? What would you do differently, and what evidence led you to that view?

## Next steps

Name one to three concrete improvements or experiments and why they matter. Put portfolio presentation tasks in the [backlog](../BACKLOG.md); keep this section about the project itself.

## Publishing check

- A reader can understand the problem, my contribution, and the outcome from the opening summary.
- Each major technical choice has a reason or a clearly stated uncertainty.
- Claims match the available evidence; unsuccessful and inconclusive results remain visible.
- Diagrams, images, and code excerpts explain something the prose alone does not.
- Links work, screenshots are readable on a phone, and dates/status are current.
